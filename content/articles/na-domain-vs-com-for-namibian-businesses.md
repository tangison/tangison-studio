Every Namibian business buying its first domain faces the same fork: the local .na, or the global .com. The decision is smaller than it feels, but one part of it regularly surprises people, so it is worth laying out plainly.

## The pricing reality

The .na country domain is administered by NA-NIC locally, and registering directly through them is the affordable route for organizations with a Namibian presence. The surprise comes through international registrars: a quick comparison of resellers shows .na registrations listed in the range of thousands of US dollars per year, among the most expensive country domains on the market, a consequence of a small registry, limited registrar competition, and pricing aimed at trademark protection rather than local commerce.

The practical implication: if your business can complete a local registration, .na is obtainable at sane local rates. If you are registering from abroad or through a foreign reseller, the same letters can cost more than the website itself.

A .com, by contrast, costs about the same anywhere in the world: a low double-digit dollar figure per year, from hundreds of competing registrars.

## What each extension signals

A .na domain says "we are here", genuinely Namibian, locally registered. For organizations selling primarily to Namibian customers, particularly in sectors where local presence is a trust signal, that statement has value. It also signals rarity: most businesses you compete with online are on something else.

A .com says nothing in particular, which is its strength. Nobody wonders whether a .com is legitimate, and no customer has ever failed to find a business because it lacked a country domain. For businesses selling across borders, or planning to, .com is the neutral default.

## The SEO question, answered honestly

Search engines treat both fine. Google's guidance has consistently been that the extension itself is a minor signal at most; what matters is content, relevance, and locality signals like a complete Google Business Profile, a Namibian phone number and address on the site, and consistent listings. A well-built .com site with clear Windhoek addressing will outrank a neglected .na site for "services in Windhoek" every time.

Country domains do get a mild boost for country-restricted searches, which matters if your market is strictly domestic. For everyone else, it is close to a wash.

## How we advise clients

The pattern we follow across our [case studies](/cases):

- **Primarily domestic, local registration feasible**: .na is a reasonable, confident choice. see a group like [Mendozer](/cases/mendozer) on its own name.
- **Any cross-border ambition, or a shorter available name on .com**: take the .com. Long-term flexibility is worth more than the local gesture.
- **The name you want is taken on one, free on the other**: the available clean name usually beats the preferred extension. A memorable .com name outworks an awkward .na one.

Whichever you choose, the rules that actually protect you are unglamorous: register the domain in your organization's name, not your developer's; keep the renewal on a card that will not expire; and renew for multiple years if the name matters, domains lost to a lapsed card are bought back at predatory prices.

Your domain is your address, not your strategy. Put the decision energy into [what the site says](/blog/seo-basics-for-namibian-businesses) once people arrive.
