Every handover we do ends the same way: we train the client's people, then step back. That habit comes from an observation repeated across years of project work in Namibia. Systems fail more often from a missing skill than from a missing part. A country that wants its infrastructure run by its own people has to grow the people.

That is why the partnership we are announcing matters to us. Tangison is working with the Oryx Polytechnic Institute on training and education, from Windhoek. This note states the scope as exactly what it covers, the same way we state everything else.

## What the Oryx Polytechnic Institute is

The institute describes itself as a new polytechnic for Namibia: research, practice, and companies that prove the work. It operates from Windhoek, is in prelaunch, and is pursuing accreditation with the NQA and the NTA. Its material is bilingual, published in English and Chinese.

Prelaunch means what it says. The institute is building its programmes now, and the accreditation work is under way. We state that plainly so no reader mistakes a young institution for an established university. What made us want to work with them is the model: research, practice, and ventures connected to each other, not kept in separate buildings.

## What the partnership covers

Three things, no more and no less.

Practitioner-taught modules. Our engineers teach from systems that shipped: the edge deployments, the AI infrastructure, the brand and product work in the case gallery. Not slides about the work, the work itself, including what went wrong on the way to shipping it.

Internship pipelines. A route for institute students into real production work, with the same review standards and accountability any team member carries. Interns touch live systems under supervision, because a portfolio built on real conditions is worth more than one built on exercises.

Joint applied research. Problems Namibian conditions actually pose: power, connectivity, dust, heat, distance. Studied together, written up, and published so other teams can use the results.

## Why a studio teaches

A studio that only ships products keeps its knowledge in the building. A studio that teaches puts it into circulation. Both the institute's model and ours come from the same idea: practice and research feed each other, and the fastest way to learn a discipline is to do it under someone who has done it before.

There is also a plainer reason. The studio is one team in Windhoek, and the country's bench of practitioners is deep enough to count. Growing it is not charity and not marketing. It is the only way the kind of work we do keeps happening here.

## Where it sits

The institute joins our client wall alongside the organisations we build for, and the work we do together will show up in Studio Notes as it lands: the modules, the internship intakes, and the research, each one published when it is real.

If you are a student or staff member at the institute, our contact page reaches the team directly. If you are building an institution that teaches from live practice, the same door applies.
